# Musical clock and synthesis engine

The public renderer is `render(instrument, options)`, returning finite float64 PCM shaped `(frames, channels)` for drum, bass or lead. The clock uses Decimal round-half-up at every event boundary, never accumulating rounded step lengths. Defaults are 120 BPM, one bar, four beats/bar, four steps/beat, 44.1 kHz, stereo, gain 0.7 and seed zero. Output ends at the exact loop boundary unless tail seconds is explicitly requested. Randomness belongs to a local seeded generator.

Patterns are whitespace/comma separated tokens, or compact drum strings. `.`/`-` rest, `x` hit, `X` accent. Note names (C4, F#3), MIDI integers or `440Hz` can form chords with `+`. A suffix `:duration:velocity:probability` overrides step duration in beats, amplitude and probability. Events contain beat, duration, notes, velocity, probability and accent. Swing delays odd numbered subdivision steps by swing times half a step. Offset is measured in beats.

Drums accept drum_patterns and drum_params dictionaries keyed by kick, snare, closed_hat, open_hat, clap, tom and rim. Kicks/toms use descending sine pitch; snare/clap combine filtered noise and tones; hats/rim use high-frequency noise/tone. Closed hats choke ringing open hats. Each voice has gain, decay, pitch, pitch_envelope, attack, tone_noise, cutoff, resonance, saturation and stereo pan controls. Humanize is maximum timing displacement in seconds; velocity_humanize is maximum amplitude displacement. Patterns repeat to fill all requested bars; explicit event lists do not repeat. Scale snapping supports chromatic, major, minor and pentatonic with MIDI or named roots. Transpose shifts oscillator notes by semitones. Bass has one voice, oscillator sine/triangle/saw/pulse, sub oscillator, cents detune, pulse width, ADSR, resonant lowpass and filter ADSR, glide, legato, accents, saturation and filter LFO. Lead has bounded polyphony with oldest voice stealing, unison and symmetric cents detune, chords, ordered/seeded arpeggios and vibrato. Oscillators use harmonic summation constrained below Nyquist, capped at 64 harmonics; this cap changes low-note timbre.

Pseudocode: parse tokens -> validate notes and event controls -> compute beat positions -> convert boundaries through exact clock -> probability gate from seeded RNG -> schedule voices -> render local ADSR and oscillator/noise -> apply voice/filter controls -> add into PCM -> sanitize finite values -> stereo duplication -> gain. Bass glides from the previous triggered pitch and suppresses attack on legato overlaps. Lead sorts scheduled note starts and trims the oldest active note when its voice limit is exceeded. Drum closed-hat starts trim active open hats. A stable second-order lowpass filters oscillators; resonance maps to bounded Q. Dynamic envelopes update coefficients in 64-frame blocks.

Limitations: oscillator stereo output is dual mono unless panned; filters update in 64-frame blocks; legato sustains envelope attack but is not a continuous analog envelope state machine; drums are procedural approximations; harmonic cap is a CPU/timbre compromise. No device or file IO belongs to this layer. Playback latency and driver selection belong to the CLI. No claim of analog hardware emulation is made.

Ties (`~`) extend the previous note duration by one subdivision without a new onset. Scale `none` aliases chromatic. Lead mode `mono` uses the one-voice scheduler and glide; poly mode does not glide. Three millisecond ramps prevent discontinuities at oscillator note boundaries and drum tails, including stolen voices and hats. Finite numeric MIDI notes are limited to 0..127, and oscillator frequencies including modulation must stay below Nyquist. Unknown renderer/preset keys and unknown per-drum controls raise errors. Global gain is applied once after voice mixing.

Renderer allocation is limited to 10,000,000 frames per call. Larger arrangements must be split into chunks. Drum parameter mappings are validated before any audio allocation or scheduling: unknown voices/keys, nonnumeric/nonfinite values, negative gains and invalid ranges fail even when that drum is not scheduled. Explicit events reject unknown fields and invalid frequencies even if probability is zero.
